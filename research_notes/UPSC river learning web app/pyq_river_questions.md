# UPSC CSE river-related previous-year questions (PYQs), 2011–2026: catalogue and pattern analysis

**How this was built.** The Prelims catalogue comes from a machine-readable copy of all 1,600 GS Paper-I questions from 2011 to 2026 (100 per year). It was extracted from the JSON data embedded in UnlockIAS year pages ([2011](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/2011) … [2026](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/2026)). That dataset tags each answer as `official_final_key`: 992 questions, mostly 2018–2025. It tags 99 questions as `official_provisional_key`, which is 2026. It leaves about 500 untagged, mostly 2011–2017. Every question was keyword-screened for rivers, water bodies, dams and related terms, using 150+ river, lake and dam names. Matches were then reviewed by hand. Question text below is UnlockIAS's transcription. Many items were cross-checked against Lotus IAS, Drishti, Chahal, IASGyan and Edukemy compilations, and the wording matches. I did **not** open the upsc.gov.in PDFs directly, so treat the wording as "verbatim per transcription".

**Tiering used in the counts**
- **CORE** means the question directly tests river knowledge: names, tributaries, sources or courses, places or sites on rivers, dams or reservoirs, interlinking, river landforms (waterfalls, canyons, deltas), river fauna, or river governance.
- **ADJ** (adjacent) means lakes, wetlands, Ramsar, glaciers or water governance where a river is not the tested fact.

## 1. Year-by-year Prelims river questions (counts + verbatim list)

### Takeaway
UPSC Prelims asked about **2–4 CORE river questions a year**: 43 CORE in 16 papers (2011–2026), about 2.7 per year. Another **30 adjacent** lake, wetland or glacier questions bring the total to 73, about 4.6 per year. Most CORE questions are static Indian-river questions on tributaries, sources, places on rivers, reservoirs and waterfalls. They are almost always in the pairs or "how many statements" format. 2026 was an outlier with 6 CORE questions, three of them framed through ancient history (Rigvedic river names, Pleistocene drainage, the Amaravati stupa on the Krishna).

**Third-party weightage figures (for triangulation)**
- UnlockIAS's own taxonomy puts 49 of 546 geography PYQs (1995–2026) in "Indian Rivers & Water Resources". It calls the topic a "perennial favourite", and it is the 4th-largest of 25 geography sub-topics. — [UnlockIAS geography](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/geography)
- UnlockIAS also says geography made up 16% of Prelims questions before 2011 and 11% since. — [UnlockIAS geography](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/geography)
- Education Province counts **23 "Indian Geography – Rivers, Lakes, Lagoons" questions out of 178 geography questions in 2011–2026 (13%)**. For 2022–2026 alone it counts 7 of 68 (10%). — [Education Province](https://educationprovince.com/geography-previous-year-questions-upsc-prelims/)
  - In the same table, world map-based questions rise from 11% to 16%, and roads, railways and ports from 4% to 10%, in the recent window.
  - Its count is lower than mine because it tags only the geography section. Mine also includes river questions filed under environment, history, art and culture, and current affairs.
- Bharat Notes splits the 2026 paper as History 13, Economy 15, S&T 15, IR 12, Geography 9, Environment 9 and Art & Culture 9. The river content in 2026 was spread across History, Art & Culture, Environment and Geography. — [Bharat Notes 2026 key](https://bharatnotes.com/prelims/2026/gs1/)
- Mrunal says that since Prelims-2016 geography has received "alternate weightage of 4MCQ-7MCQ-4MCQ-7MCQs". He notes that "Low profile tributaries of famous rivers [were] asked e.g. Godavari, Bramhaputra, Teesta in 2015, 2016 and 2017 respectively". He also writes "In 2019 UPSC examination gone crazier than usual: 2 MCQs on Indian rivers", and that 2019 included a "Glacier vs river pair question". — [Mrunal 2021 geography key](https://mrunal.org/2021/11/answerkey-upsc-prelims-2021-geography-mcq.html)

**Negative findings (keyword scan of all 1,600 Prelims questions, 2011–2026)** — [UnlockIAS year pages](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/2025)
- No Prelims question mentions the **Indus Waters Treaty, Ken–Betwa, the Nile or GERD, Mekong dams, or Teesta water-sharing**.
- "Namami Gange" never appears by name. River-governance questions instead asked about the **NGRBA** (2014 Q96 and 2016 Q23).
- The only Mekong items are 2015 Q64 (Mekong-Ganga Cooperation membership) and 2020 Q68 (which sea the Mekong flows into).
- There is no "arrange north-to-south" river question. The only ordering item is 2024 Q6, a west-to-east sequence of Ganga tributaries.

### Inferences
- Rivers are a **reliable 2–4 marks-equivalent** each year (each GS question carries 2 marks, so roughly 4–8 marks), and the 2026 spike (6 CORE plus 5 adjacent) means UPSC now spreads river content across subjects. An app that teaches rivers only as "geography" will miss the history and culture framings: Rigvedic names, Mahajanapadas, Vijayanagara dams, stupas or caves in river valleys or gorges, and places on rivers.
- The core static syllabus that recurs:
  1. Tributary membership and confluence order for the Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra and Godavari systems
  2. Sources (Eastern Ghats versus elsewhere; Tibetan versus Sikkim origin)
  3. Places, heritage sites and protected areas on or along named rivers
  4. Reservoirs and dams mapped to rivers and states
  5. Waterfalls and canyons mapped to rivers and regions
  6. River–lake relationships
  7. River fauna (dolphins, gharial, mahseer)
  8. Flow-into or outlet facts for major world rivers
- World rivers appear about once every 2–3 years, almost always as a basin, outlet or lake fact, never as a dam or treaty item.

## 2. Mains river and water questions: GS1, GS2, GS3 and the Geography Optional

### Takeaway
GS1 asks a river or water question **almost every year, usually one or two at 10–15 marks**. The recurring themes are interlinking of rivers, floods (urban and Ganga-plain), water scarcity or stress, groundwater, Himalayan glaciers and water, deltas, and the Ganga basin. GS3 adds Ganga pollution and NMCG, river linking and environment, industrial river pollution, dam failures, urban floods, cloudbursts and wetlands. The Geography Optional goes deepest. It asks about east- and west-flowing rivers, Peninsular drainage, river capture, river linking, Ganga-plain floods, river water quality and fluvial geomorphology. Its compulsory map question includes a river almost every year.

## 3. Recurring question formats

### Takeaway
The dominant Prelims format is **matching pairs or rows, scored as "how many are correctly matched"**. Next come **multi-select membership** ("which of the above are tributaries of X / rise from Y / flow through Z") and **statements about one river or lake**. Rarer forms are sequence ordering (W→E), identify-the-river-from-clues, causal or "why" items, and "what is common to" items. Since 2022–23, UPSC's general shift to "how many pairs/statements" makes partial knowledge useless for elimination.

### Inferences
Format-to-feature mapping for the app:
- **Pairs and rows** call for flash-cards or graph quizzes over attribute triples: river ↔ tributary ↔ state ↔ dam/reservoir ↔ waterfall ↔ region ↔ city/heritage site ↔ glacier ↔ fauna ↔ ancient name.
- **Membership** calls for "tick all that apply" drills by basin and by state.
- **Sequence** calls for confluence-order drills along the Ganga (W→E) and the Indus (Panjnad).
- **Clues** call for 20-questions-style identification by drainage type (antecedent), countries crossed, source and distributaries.
- **Reversed-pair traps** call for training on direction and orientation (which end is in India).
- Because "how many are correct" removes elimination, the app must test **every** attribute in a row, not just the most salient one.

### Gaps
- No official UPSC format taxonomy exists; the tally is mine.
- I found no Prelims question in a "locate on the given map" image format (UPSC Prelims has no map images). "Map-based" here means place-knowledge questions.

## 4. Most frequent sub-types and what aspirants find hardest

### Takeaway
The most frequent sub-types are:
1. Tributary or system membership, with Ganga, Indus, Brahmaputra, Godavari and Teesta each appearing
2. River ↔ place, heritage site or protected area
3. Reservoir, dam or waterfall ↔ river or state
4. River ↔ lake or wetland relationships

The hardest are questions that hinge on **precise hydrological semantics**: "joins directly", "directly feeds", "formed by meandering", "rises from the Eastern Ghats". Next are **lesser-known tributaries and sources**, and **multi-attribute rows** where one attribute is subtly wrong (Gersoppa–Netravati, Dhuandhar–Malwa).

### Inferences
Highest-value "confusion clusters" for the app to drill explicitly:
- **Indus system:** the Jhelum and Ravi join the Chenab; the Chenab joins the Sutlej to form the Panjnad, which joins the Indus.
- **"Feeds directly" versus via distributary or overflow:** Kolleru; Teesta→Jamuna.
- **Origin traps:** Teesta (Tso Lhamo/Pahunri) versus Brahmaputra (Chemayungdung).
- **Eastern Ghats-origin rivers** (Nagavali, Vamsadhara) versus Chota Nagpur-origin rivers (Brahmani, Subarnarekha).
- **Glacier → river:** Milam→Gori Ganga, Zemu→Teesta, Bara Shigri→Chenab, Bandarpunch→Yamuna, Siachen→Nubra.
- **Waterfall → river → region triples.**
- **Reservoir → state**, especially for inter-state rivers.
- **Heritage site → river:** Hampi→Tungabhadra, Pandharpur→Chandrabhaga (Bhima), Ajanta→Waghora, Amaravati→Krishna.
- **Ancient → modern names:** Vitasta = Jhelum, Asikni = Chenab, Parushni = Ravi, Vipas = Beas, Sutudri = Sutlej, Sadanira = Gandak.

- I found no systematic survey of aspirant-perceived difficulty by river sub-type. The difficulty evidence comes from coaching tags (Mrunal, Bharat Notes) and from answer-key disputes.
- Reddit and Quora threads were not retrieved.

- 90 candidates challenged 2026 Prelims questions before the Central Administrative Tribunal (CAT); the specific questions were not identified. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/feature/90-candidates-upsc-prelims-questions-6-mps-support/3066195/)
- Bharat Notes flags only one 2026 question as disputed (Q89), which is not a river question. — [Bharat Notes](https://bharatnotes.com/prelims/2026/gs1/)
- Mrunal's list of 16 MCQs where the 2020 official key and coaching keys differed contained no river item that my keyword grep could find. — [Mrunal 2020 discrepancies](https://mrunal.org/2021/11/upsc-prelims-2020-answerkey-error-discrepancy.html)

### Inferences
- These disputes come from (i) ambiguous verbs ("joins directly", "feeds directly"), (ii) contested landform genesis (Kanwar), and (iii) transcription or key anomalies.
- For the app, each such item should carry an "examiner's answer vs. textbook nuance" note and a citation. This is valuable content because coaching material itself is inconsistent.

### Gaps
- The official UPSC key PDFs were not opened directly. The 2019 Hampi item especially needs a direct check on upsc.gov.in.
- I did not search the 2024 waterfall question deeply for disputes; I found no reports of one.
- Reddit and Quora threads were not reviewed.

## 6. Links to current affairs (rivers in the news)

### Takeaway
Prelims river questions are **mostly static**. When they are news-linked, the hook is usually an Indian project or policy event: a river-linking project, a tiger reserve or Ramsar designation, a bridge, or a river-conservation body. World items are physical-geography facts about basins or lakes that were in the news. They are **not** treaty or dam-dispute questions. Indus Waters Treaty, Ken–Betwa, GERD and Teesta water-sharing appear only in Mains (Indus treaty, GS1 2016) or not at all.

### Cited Findings
- **2016 Q85 (Godavari–Krishna linking):** tied to the Pattiseema Lift Irrigation Project in Andhra Pradesh. — [IASGyan](https://www.iasgyan.in/prelims-previous-year-papers/geography-pyq)
- **2015 Q64 (Mekong-Ganga Cooperation):** an IR or regional-grouping hook (India plus Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia and Vietnam). — [Edukemy](https://edukemy.com/blog/geography-previous-year-questions-upsc-prelims-2013-2022/)
- **2026 Q22:** Madhav National Park was declared "India's 58th Tiger Reserve" on 9 March 2025. Sakhya Sagar, a reservoir created by damming the Manier river, was added to the Ramsar List on 7 January 2022. — [Bharat Notes](https://bharatnotes.com/prelims/2026/gs1/)
- **2026 Q86:** Maitri Setu over the Feni was "inaugurated 9 March 2021". The question used a reversed-pair trap. — [Bharat Notes](https://bharatnotes.com/prelims/2026/gs1/)
- **2026 Q29 (Lake Turkana):** the explanation links it to the "Gibe III dam controversy (Ethiopia)" and the "World Heritage in Danger list". — [Bharat Notes](https://bharatnotes.com/prelims/2026/gs1/)
- **2026 Q92 (Mangdechhu hydroelectric project, Bhutan):** a neighbourhood-aid current-affairs item. — [UnlockIAS 2026](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/2026)
- **2025 (UN international years pairs question; Q56 in Max IAS's numbering):** the years included "2025-International Year of Glaciers' Preservation". — [Max IAS 2025](https://maxias.in/blog/upsc-prelims-2025-key-with-solutions-analysis/)
- **2022 Q23 (Lake Faguibine, Mali):** the lake was "used to be fed by annual flooding from the Niger River" and has been dry since the 1970s. — [Edukemy](https://edukemy.com/blog/geography-previous-year-questions-upsc-prelims-2013-2022/)
- **River governance:** NGRBA was asked in 2014 (chaired by the PM) and 2016 (features); Namami Gange and NMCG in Mains GS3 2015. — [UnlockIAS 2014](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/2014); [UnlockIAS 2016](https://www.unlockias.in/upsc-prelims-pyq/2016); [Inclusive IAS](https://inclusiveias.com/upsc-mains-environment-previous-year-questions/)
- **Mains news-linkage examples:**
  - Urban floods: 2016, 2020 (Hyderabad, Pune), 2024.
  - Cloudbursts: 2016 (Uttarakhand), 2022.
  - Dam failures: 2023.
  - Seawater intrusion: 2025. Legacy IAS describes it as "climate-linked & coastal hazards are the fastest-rising trend".
  - Sources: [Legacy IAS Disaster Management](https://www.legacyias.com/upsc-disaster-management-pyqs-2013-2025/); [ForumIAS](https://forumias.com/blog/geography-previous-year-questions-upsc-mains-examination/)
- **River interlinking stays in the news:** Anantam IAS reports the PM's 51st PRAGATI meeting asking states to resolve inter-state water disputes, and that "the Ken-Betwa project should serve as a model". It flags Ken–Betwa as a Prelims and Mains topic. — [Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/?p=27851)

### Inferences
- The current-affairs layer is best built as a "river in news → static anchor" card: Pattiseema leads to Godavari and Krishna geography; Maitri Setu to the Feni; Madhav NP to Sakhya Sagar and the Manier.
- Treaty and dispute news (IWT, Teesta, Ken–Betwa, Mekong, GERD) is more Mains-relevant. For Prelims it should be taught mainly as the underlying geography: tributary allocation (eastern vs western rivers), basin countries, source and outlet.

### Gaps
- I did not systematically map each year's news events against the questions, so this is anecdotal pairing.
- Whether Ken–Betwa or the IWT abeyance will appear in Prelims 2027 is speculative and is excluded.

## 7. State PSCs, CDS/NDA, CAPF and SSC: do they ask similar river questions?

### Takeaway
Yes. River and drainage questions are a staple of the other UPSC exams (CDS, NDA, CAPF), of SSC and of state PSCs, usually in a simpler one-liner style (origin, tributary, dam–river, longest river). Hard frequency data is scarce.

### Cited Findings
- A cross-exam PYQ tracker counts **118 "Drainage Systems" questions** in UPSC exams over 2011–2025:

| Exam | Questions | Last asked |
|---|---|---|
| UPSC CSE | 47 | 2024 |
| CDSE | 31 | 2025 |
| NDA | 26 | 2024 |
| CAPF | 14 | 2022 |

  The tracker names 2018 as the "Peak Year". — [Marks/getmarks](https://www.getmarks.app/upsc/geography/indian-physical-geography/drainage-systems)
- For SSC GD: "Every year, 2 to 4 direct questions are asked about rivers, tributaries, river origin, direction of flow, and special river facts." Left-bank versus right-bank tributary questions are flagged as a common trap. — [PW (SSC GD)](https://www.pw.live/ssc/exams/ssc-gd-gk-indian-river-system)
- State PSC examples:
  - BPSC 2024 asked which drainage system drains Bihar. — [upscsociology.in](https://upscsociology.in/?p=34472)
  - BPSC 2019 (Ganga-basin tributary), UPPSC 2014 (South Indian drainage patterns) and HPSC 2022 (ranking rivers by length) are cited. — [Edukemy drainage notes](https://edukemy.com/blog/upsc-ncert-notes-geography-drainage-system-of-india/)

### Inferences
- An app that covers UPSC CSE river depth would also serve CDS, NDA, CAPF, SSC and state PSC users, which widens the market.
- Those exams lean towards one-liner recall (origin, longest, dam–river, left- or right-bank). Basic drill modes would serve them, with UPSC-style multi-attribute "how many correct" modes as the advanced tier.
- State PSCs add state-specific rivers (for example Bihar's Ghaghara, Gandak and Kosi for BPSC), which suggests state filters.

### Gaps
- I found **no reliable year-wise count** of river questions for UPPSC, BPSC, MPPSC, RPSC, SSC CGL or CDS individually. The figures above are a single tracker (for UPSC exams) and a single coaching estimate (for SSC GD).
- Measuring the true frequency would need parsing each exam's papers, which was not done here.
